Design principles

How the tool is built

Research-based constructs

Observable relationship processes

The core asks about communication, connection under stress, pursue-withdraw patterns, regulation, repair, acceptance, everyday connection, and teamwork. The questions are original and are not copied from validated scales.

Relationship map

Patterns, not verdicts

The couple map looks for interaction loops and differences in how each partner experiences the same relationship. It does not produce a compatibility score or decide who is right.

Jungian depth lens

Questions about the unconscious

Persona, shadow, projection, emotional complexes, the four-part relationship map, and individuation are used as guided questions. The tool does not pretend that an unconscious process can be measured directly by a self-report score.

How to read this tool

Limits and evidence

What the numbers mean

Scores are simple averages of original questions, from 1 to 5. They describe what you reported, not a diagnosis. There is no score that means something is definitely a problem. The scores are for comparison and reflection, not diagnosis. A difference between partners means the same relationship is being experienced differently. It does not tell you which partner is correct.

How pattern cards are surfaced

The tool uses simple discussion rules to decide what to show first. There is no score that means something is definitely a problem, and the results are for comparison and reflection, not diagnosis. For example, a pattern may be surfaced when a person answers around “often” across the matching questions, or when a relationship resource is reported around “rarely to sometimes.” These rules only decide which conversation prompt to show first.

Why Jungian ideas are mostly qualitative here

Shadow, projection, complexes, anima/animus, and individuation concern processes that may be partly unconscious. A person cannot directly measure their own unconscious by checking boxes. The Jungian sections therefore use questions to generate hypotheses that can be tested against lived experience.

What this tool does not copy

This tool does not reproduce or claim the validation of instruments such as the Couple Satisfaction Index, Experiences in Close Relationships measures, Communication Patterns Questionnaire, or other proprietary or validated scales. Its questions and scoring are original.

Privacy

The assessment code does not send questionnaire answers to a server, use analytics, set cookies, or use local storage. A Share Code can include summary results from optional modules and the culture/life-context factors you chose to share; free-text context notes stay local. Standard browser and web-hosting metadata may still exist when the page is hosted online. Refreshing or closing a browser is not a reliable privacy method. Use the Clear all responses button if you want to clear the current page.

Selected foundations

Jungian: C. G. Jung, Psychological Types; Aion; The Psychology of the Transference; Marriage as a Psychological Relationship.

Couple therapy: Integrative Behavioral Couple Therapy; Emotionally Focused Couple Therapy; behavioral couple therapy; demand-withdraw research; mentalization-based couple work; relationship repair and responsiveness research.

Measurement: Research on relationship satisfaction, adult attachment, communication patterns, and routine outcome monitoring informed the architecture, but the present questions are original and not a substitute for validated measures.

Before using this tool together Safety checklist Read this first if you have concerns about fear, control, violence, sexual pressure, or privacy.

Do not complete this questionnaire as a shared couple exercise, and consider not completing it at all on this device, if you cannot answer freely and safely. This tool assumes that both people can disagree, say no, set boundaries, and speak honestly without fear of punishment.

It is better not to use this questionnaire with your partner if any of the following apply:

  • You are afraid of how your partner may react to your answers, disagreement, boundaries, or a decision not to continue.
  • Your partner controls or closely monitors your movements, money, work or study, social contacts, phone, messages, accounts, or location.
  • There have been threats, intimidation, stalking, property damage, harm to pets, blocking exits, restraint, choking or strangulation, physical violence, or threats involving weapons.
  • There has been sexual pressure, sexual activity without free consent, or punishment after you say no.
  • You fear retaliation if you seek help, tell someone what is happening, or try to leave.
  • You are being pressured or required to complete this tool, show your answers, or share your Share Code.
  • You suspect that the device or account you are using is being monitored and seeing this page could put you at risk.
  • You cannot speak honestly because the other person may use your answers against you later.
In these situations, the problem is not ordinary miscommunication. A joint relationship exercise can increase risk or give a controlling partner more information to use. Consider private support from a qualified professional or a specialist domestic, family, or sexual violence service instead. If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services.

If you use parts of this tool only for private reflection, consider using a safer device that the other person cannot access. Quick hide does not erase browser history or other digital traces.
